## The story

Maia is a nine-year-old Black girl at an American school in the 1970s. Adopted into a white family, she faces bias in class because of her race and her gender, until her friends stand beside her and they speak up together. The book is written for readers aged 9 to 13.


## Turning research into a story

- **Research becomes plot.** Every panel maps to a research idea, such as intersectionality, racialized ideas of childhood innocence, and children's agency, with footnotes that connect the theory to the story.
- **Familiar settings.** The classroom, playtime, and the playground let children recognize unfairness in situations they know.
- **Emotion first.** We prioritized empathy and identification over polished visuals.
- **Children at the center.** The story moves from being silenced to speaking up together, showing what children can do.

## My role

This was a group project, and I was responsible for the art. I illustrated the entire book by hand in Procreate, from character design to all 24 panels. The project earned a first-class grade.

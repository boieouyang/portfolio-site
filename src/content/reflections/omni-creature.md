For this project, I interviewed my classmate Yiyang for about ten minutes to learn about different parts of her identity and interests. Three things stood out from our conversation: she loves playing Apex Legends, especially the character Rampart; she really likes broccoli; and she is from Hangzhou and has a strong connection to West Lake.

## From interview to design

I wanted to combine these three elements into one creature rather than simply placing three symbols next to each other. I used Rampart as the main character, a simplified West Lake landscape as the background, and a large broccoli at the front. The broccoli is attached to a cylindrical structure extending from Rampart, making it a more playful and three-dimensional part of the design.

![The finished creature, with the character in front of a West Lake landscape and a broccoli extending forward](/images/UtUX8XHkJZXyitW63auKXo8Pmsk.webp)

The three identities also occupy different layers of the object: Rampart is the character, West Lake becomes the environment, and the broccoli becomes an object extending toward the viewer.

## Prototyping the kerf bend

The part I was most unsure about was the kerf bend, since this was a new technique for me. I first tested the pattern with cardboard. It could bend, but the cardboard was too soft to hold the shape well, so I made another small prototype using wood.

![Kerf-cut wooden test strips laid side by side](/images/IeuV5RGaJgNtRr1Nvgf86qOk4hY.webp)

The wood worked much better, but when I tried to bend it into a cylinder, I found that the joint was difficult to keep closed. I asked others for suggestions and used wood glue and clamps to hold it together.

![A clamp holding the kerf-bent wood closed while the glue dries](/images/6mEeSXlD0EAUrambPuggYQ4Zr2I.webp)

I was still worried that the cylinder would become unstable after removing the clamps. I measured its diameter with a caliper and laser-cut several wooden rings to fit around it. The rings worked much better than I expected and helped the cylinder keep its shape.

![Measuring the cylinder's diameter with a caliper](/images/GB16PHh9oKKD6IUCUU5lGWhguo.webp)

![Holding the cylinder with laser-cut rings fitted around it](/images/55cK8J9CDHUZyIAzR8qpV3E0.webp)

This was probably my favorite problem-solving moment in the project, because the rings started as a practical fix but eventually became part of the visual design.

## Press fits and assembly

I used press-fit joints to assemble different parts of the creature, including the base, the broccoli, and the circular rings. The base lets the main character and the West Lake background stand without glue, while the rings help support the cylindrical structure.

![Side view of the assembled creature, its layers press-fit into the base](/images/w0Bl7AuLCIq8nUeYGWkhK0LGqk.webp)

Layering multiple pieces also helped turn what began as flat plywood into an object with much more depth.

## Laser cutting

One thing that did not go as planned was the laser-cutting time. My design was quite large and used almost the entire sheet of wood. It also included many detailed lines, especially on Rampart.

More importantly, I placed different designs far apart on the sheet. While watching the laser cutter, I realized that the laser head had to travel long distances between different areas, which made the whole process even slower.

![The full sheet in the laser cutter, with pieces spread far apart](/images/nh102kevmbE27jSyHYgqm1Zl4cw.webp)

Next time, I would make the overall design slightly smaller, simplify some unnecessary lines, and arrange the pieces closer together to reduce both material use and cutting time.

## Final reflection

This was a useful activity for learning laser cutting because I had to think beyond simply cutting a 2D shape. I learned how kerf bending, press fits, layering, and measurement can turn flat pieces of wood into a larger 3D structure.

The biggest lesson for me was that laser cutting involves a lot of iteration. My first solution did not always work, especially with the kerf-bent cylinder, but prototyping, measuring, asking others, and making small adjustments gradually led to the final result. If I did this project again, I would spend more time optimizing my file before cutting, but I am happy with how the three parts of Yiyang's identity came together in one creature.
